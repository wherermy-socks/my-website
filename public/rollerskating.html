Rollerskating Info

How to start rollerskating?

I started on inline skates when I was in elementary school, so it might feel like I would be the worst person to give advice but I'll give you two tips I still use to this day: 1] get as much protection as you need to feel comfortable and 2] start slow.

When I say whatever you need to feel comfortable, I truly do mean it; start on grass, with a helmet, knee pads, elbow pads, wrist protectors, and even a butt pad or cushion. This wasn't my experience with skates [skates were an 'accesible' hobby to my family, in the sense that if you could afford a pair, everything else was an extra expense and not absolutely necessary]. If you're a bit more open to bruises and scratches, then a good helmet, wrist protectors and knee pads are my go-to.

When I say start slow, I mean learn the basics online. Don't just try to copy walking on wheels; look up how to cruise forward, how to stop, how to fall safely (yes, this is a thing you have to practice!). It's also a sport, meaning you might get winded or tired fast. Keep at it! It's a fun and healthy way to stay active. My favorite places to practice are uncared for basketball courts that no one uses [use your public third-spaces!].

What pair should I buy?

As I stated above, rollerksating was my family's 'accesible' hobby, meaning I've only ever had two pairs in my life. Adjustable inlines 'til I was 11, and quads when I left highschool. I'm not the best person to dictate what you should buy and what you shouldn't. Rule of thumb: inlines for speed, quads for tricks on 'nicer' flooring. You can do both with both; you can use them for long-distance, for artistic dance or for riding the skatepark bowl. But those are the main distinguishers and will affect what mainly comes with the pair. I've seen people buy them second-hand too, but I've never tried it myself.
